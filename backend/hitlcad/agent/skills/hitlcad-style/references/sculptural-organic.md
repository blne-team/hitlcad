# Sculptural organic

Use when the brief favors gesture, soft masses, irregular growth or an expressive character. The
amount of asymmetry and exaggeration follows the human's intended expression.

## Geometric vocabulary

Establish a gesture and hierarchy of masses. Relate secondary forms through controlled transitions
and deliberate negative spaces. Use asymmetry to suggest movement or character while preserving
functional contact and exact interfaces. Vary one family of shapes coherently instead of adding
unrelated bumps and decorative appendages.

Possible controls: mass ratio, lean, twist, flare, offset and transition softness. Keep mating
cylinders and installation depths independent of expressive deformation.

## Failure and tradeoffs

Overlapping primitives may leave visible lumps or unwanted disconnected pieces. Fine appendages
can create manufacturing problems. A compelling side view may conceal an awkward front or back.
Increasing a mass can change real balance and joint demands, even if it improves visual balance.

## Evaluate

Inspect the silhouette and spaces between masses before texture or colour. Review back/top views,
part boundaries and the chosen process. Use `hitlcad-form` for implementation reasoning and the
backend contract for manifold geometry. Ask the human to judge the intended expression when needed.

This is an original vocabulary using the proportion, balance and form concepts in
[Getty's formal analysis resources](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/formal_analysis.html).
Recognition and emotional character remain judgments to evaluate, not automatic geometric verdicts.
