# Streamlined

Use when a direction of motion, action or visual flow should organize the object. A flowing shell
can be appropriate even when aerodynamics are not a functional requirement.

## Geometric vocabulary

Choose a principal axis and a clear progression of cross-sections. Use taper, controlled bulge and
continuous transitions to relate major masses. Integrate seams and openings with that progression,
while preserving access and real functional clearances. Keep secondary volumes subordinate or make
their interruption deliberate.

Possible controls: taper, section aspect ratio, bulge location, transition length and curvature at
the working end. Expose section relationships rather than dozens of unrelated profile coordinates.

## Failure and tradeoffs

Smooth external surfaces can hide an impractical split or inaccessible interior. Tiny transition
radii can create pinches; excessive curvature may increase support or tooling complexity. A curved
surface is not necessarily aerodynamic, ergonomic or easy to manufacture.

## Evaluate

Inspect silhouette continuity and section progression from several views. Check walls at transitions
and preserve internal interfaces. Review seam and opening placement against the intended direction.
Describe aerodynamic or fluid-performance benefits only with appropriate evidence.

This proposed vocabulary applies the visual transformation approach described in
[MIT's shape grammar course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-540-introduction-to-shape-grammars-i-fall-2018/pages/syllabus/).
It is a geometric design direction, not a validated flow model.
