# Quiet functional

Use when the brief calls for clarity, restraint or a calm object that fits into its environment.
This direction still needs deliberate proportion and detail.

## Geometric vocabulary

Choose a dominant volume and a small family of edge treatments. Align openings, seams and controls
with useful datums. Use spacing and depth to distinguish working surfaces from the background.
Give necessary service boundaries a clean expression. Keep gripping and operating cues recognizable.

Possible controls: overall slenderness, corner radius, border width, seam setback and control recess.
Keep critical contact dimensions independent of global scaling.

## Failure and tradeoffs

Removing cues can make an object difficult to open or operate. Hidden hardware may require awkward
assembly access. A broad uninterrupted surface can expose distortion or inconsistent transitions.
Do not erase necessary features merely to simplify the silhouette.

## Evaluate

Check whether the intended action is recognizable, details align with the construction, and the
object retains its character without a special colour treatment. Compare the main and secondary
features at a normal viewing size. Ask the human about clarity and desired restraint when those
preferences remain open.

Source: [Dieter Rams's principles at Vitsœ](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design) discusses
usefulness, intelligibility, longevity and care in detail. This guide is our CAD interpretation;
Rams's preferences are not requirements for every design style.
