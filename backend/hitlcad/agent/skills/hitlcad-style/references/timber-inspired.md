# Timber construction inspired

Use when the human asks for a visual or construction language informed by timber architecture or
joinery. Identify the particular reference tradition if historical specificity matters.

## Geometric vocabulary

Organize members into repeated bays. Articulate intersections and distinguish primary members from
secondary infill. Consider layered joints, shoulders, expressed tenons or assembled bracket-like
transitions where they serve the object. Let assembly sequence influence what is visible.

Possible controls: module pitch, member section, reveal, shoulder depth and infill setback. A real
joint's engagement and fit remain engineering values, independent of decorative exaggeration.

## Failure and tradeoffs

An applied motif can become disconnected from construction. Wood grain, moisture response and
traditional joint behavior do not transfer automatically to polymer or metal. Thin scaled-down
tenons may be unsuitable, and overly visible joints may collect dirt or impede use. Attribute a
specific tradition rather than describing all regional practices as one aesthetic.

## Evaluate

Explain the assembly order, how joints are retained and which features are expressive. Inspect
member hierarchy and junctions in multiple views. Verify the actual material/process interfaces
and leave untested strength claims open.

Source: [UNESCO: Chinese timber-framed craftsmanship](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chinese-traditional-architectural-craftsmanship-for-timber-framed-structures-00223?RL=00223)
documents prefabricated timber components and tenon-based construction. The product-scale vocabulary
above is an adaptation; it does not certify a historical reconstruction or structural performance.
