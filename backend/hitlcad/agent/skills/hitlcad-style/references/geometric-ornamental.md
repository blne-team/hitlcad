# Geometric ornamental

Use when repeated motifs, relief or perforation are central to the brief. The pattern should have
an organizing rule that can generate meaningful variation.

## Geometric vocabulary

Choose a motif and transformations: translation, rotation, reflection, subdivision, offset or a
controlled change of scale. Define borders and end conditions with the rule. Decide whether the
motif is cut through, raised, recessed or formed by actual members. Reserve material around joints,
contact surfaces and mounting hardware.

Possible controls: pitch, motif size, count, relief depth, border width and progression. Preserve
minimum webs and meaningful parameter relationships. Exact geometric pattern rules should not be
confused with universal aesthetic ratios.

## Failure and tradeoffs

Patterning every surface can destroy hierarchy or make cleaning difficult. Cutouts can disconnect
islands or weaken attachment regions. Relief may create undercuts or support demand. Open area does
not by itself establish useful cooling, sound transmission or structural efficiency.

## Evaluate

Review the pattern at the whole-object and feature scales. Check borders, interrupted motifs,
minimum webs and the smallest supported variant. The vent-field code in `hitlcad-form` illustrates
repetition; adapt the geometry to the actual process and requested character.

Source: [MIT: Introduction to Shape Grammars](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-540-introduction-to-shape-grammars-i-fall-2018/pages/syllabus/)
describes visual rules and recursive transformation. Our parameters and manufacturing reviews are
applications of that approach, not rules that guarantee attractive or feasible ornament.
