# Exposed structure

Use when the brief values visible construction, modularity or an articulated mechanical character.
Make the relationship between members, panels and joints understandable.

## Geometric vocabulary

Establish a frame and identify what its members actually support. Relate repeated ribs or mounting
stations to the functional layout. Group visible fasteners consistently and give them usable tool
access. Articulate panel boundaries and junctions with a common depth or edge treatment.

Possible controls: bay pitch, member depth, panel setback, rib spacing and joint reveal. Tie them
to real attachment geometry where appropriate, while keeping the distinct parameters understandable.

## Failure and tradeoffs

Decorative ribs can imply support they do not provide. Extra joints add tolerance and assembly work.
Exposed hardware and edges may be unsuitable in contact regions. A uniformly dense frame can obscure
controls or make all features compete for attention.

## Evaluate

Trace the actual support and assembly relationships. Inspect end conditions, panel removal and
tool approach. Review the structural rhythm in several views. State any unverified stiffness or
load assumptions separately from the visual impression.

This is a proposed form grammar using the frame, boundary and access patterns in `hitlcad-design`.
For the generative approach, see [MIT's shape grammar course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-540-introduction-to-shape-grammars-i-fall-2018/pages/syllabus/).
The specific vocabulary here is original design guidance, not a historical style definition.
