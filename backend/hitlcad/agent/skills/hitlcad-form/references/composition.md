# Composing three-dimensional form

Use to resolve an awkward shape or to establish a coherent form before surface details.

## Inspect relationships

| Question | Useful adjustment | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is seen first? | Change relative mass, contour, depth or contrast | A minor fastener boss dominating the product |
| Is visual weight deliberate? | Move or reshape a secondary mass | Mistaking visual balance for physical stability |
| Do parts belong together? | Relate edge treatment, seam direction and spacing | Making every part identical despite different roles |
| Is repetition organized? | Derive pitch, margins and end conditions together | Clipped last motifs or arbitrary leftover spaces |
| Does empty space have a role? | Preserve a hand opening, service path or visual pause | Filling every gap with ribs or decoration |

Check masses first, then secondary features, then details. For a figurine this means gesture and
head/body relationship before facial features. For a machine this means body, handle and working end
before screw recesses or surface grooves.

## Develop transitions with purpose

Use a common edge-treatment family where it serves the design. Choose exceptions for contact,
manufacturing or focal emphasis. A radius should fit the available wall and adjoining features.
Adding fillets everywhere can consume thin material, change interfaces and obscure meaningful edges.

A transition between two volumes can be abrupt, stepped, chamfered or continuous. Choose based on
the intended relationship: a detachable module may benefit from a clear step, while a grip-to-body
transition may call for a flowing contour. Tangent continuity can remove a visible kink; it does not
by itself establish an attractive reflection or comfortable contact.

## Control repetition and margins

For `n` slots of width `s` on pitch `p`, the total occupied width is `(n - 1) * p + s`.
Compare that with the available panel width after borders and protected features. Center the field
or deliberately align it to a datum; do not adjust the final slot independently to make it fit.

The complete enclosure example linked from `hitlcad-design` implements this relationship. Changing
pitch while preserving count changes the field width; changing count can change both appearance and
web thickness. Neither change establishes sufficient ventilation.

## Review at several scales

Inspect the whole object at a small displayed size for hierarchy, a normal view for proportions and
a close view for joins and transitions. Use consistent framing when comparing alternatives. A
beautiful close-up of one feature cannot settle the whole design.

## Source and adaptation

[Getty's Principles of Design](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/principles_design.pdf)
introduces balance, emphasis, movement, repetition, proportion, rhythm, variety and unity. The CAD
questions and geometric decisions above are our application of that visual vocabulary, not numerical
rules supplied by Getty.
